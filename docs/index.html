A better image fit is not automatically a better robot state.

KineSync-GS separates correction proposals from state-changing authority. A kinematically bound Gaussian renderer estimates a bounded whole-state correction; cross-view or temporal evidence verifies the proposal; unsupported updates fall back to the incoming measurement.

Proposal→Evidence→Verified update/Fallback

One contract for spatial and temporal synchronization

Different estimators expose the same auditable state interface to Gaussian rendering, simulation, and downstream policies.

01

Bounded proposal

Forward kinematics binds every Gaussian to its robot link. Multi-view inverse rendering estimates a coordinated joint correction within explicit bounds.

02

Independent evidence

Single-view estimates, visual gain, and cross-view disagreement evaluate spatial proposals. Correlated image and joint motion evaluate temporal offsets.

03

Atomic state update

Fixed verification thresholds admit the complete proposal or preserve the original measurement. The decision and its evidence are recorded together.

Precise correction without uncontrolled state changes

Matched controlled, recorded-real, temporal, and online evaluations test both state accuracy and update behavior.

01

Whole-state verification resolves conflicting views.

Across 48 held-out Franka conditions, qMAE falls from 0.590° to 0.322°. Verification accepts all 12 useful proposals without a harmful spatial update.

02

Synchronized state improves real manipulation.

Online PiPER reaches 3.07 mrad qMAE with 0.36% harmful updates. Under the same policy, task success rises from 58.33% to 71.67% over 240 paired episodes.

From controlled Gaussian state recovery to real manipulation

The evaluation spans a Franka Gaussian asset, recorded and online PiPER observations, two D455 views, and an external bimanual simulator interface.
